Build Vocabulary by Using Words Precisely

FactForge · 1 min read

Learning a new word is useful when it helps you express a distinction you previously struggled to make. A longer word is not automatically a better word, especially if your reader must stop to translate the sentence.

Choose unfamiliar terms from material you actually read. Write a short definition in your own language and create an example. Check the word in context before using it publicly, because similar dictionary definitions can hide different connotations.

Return to the word later and see whether you can use it naturally. Do not replace simple language only to make writing sound more educated. The strongest vocabulary gives you options: you can be precise when a specialist term is necessary and clear when an ordinary one is enough. Communication succeeds when the reader understands the point, not when the sentence advertises how many terms you know.
