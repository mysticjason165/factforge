Consider More Than the Best-Case Outcome

FactForge · 1 min read

A plan can sound easy when it describes only what happens if everything works. Add two more versions: an ordinary outcome and a difficult one. This can expose assumptions that the enthusiastic version leaves hidden.

For a small project, ask what happens if it takes longer, attracts fewer users, or requires more support. Estimate only what you can reasonably estimate and label guesses. The point is not to invent precise probabilities for situations you barely understand.

Use the scenarios to choose limits and fallback actions. You might reduce the first version or delay an expense until demand is clearer. Thinking about uncertainty does not mean abandoning the idea. It helps you choose a version you can still handle when the result is less convenient than the one you pictured at the start.
