Set a Work Boundary People Can Plan Around

FactForge · 1 min read

A boundary is easier to understand when it describes a specific arrangement. Saying you need more balance may be true, but it leaves other people guessing about what will change. State the availability you can realistically maintain.

For example, explain when you check routine messages and how genuinely urgent issues should be handled. Consider your role and any commitments already in place. If the current workload cannot fit the agreed hours, bring that conflict into the conversation rather than silently trying to absorb it.

Repeat the arrangement consistently and review exceptions when they begin to become normal. You do not have to justify every private hour with a productive activity. Clear boundaries support planning on both sides. Their success also depends on the environment, so difficulty enforcing them is not automatically evidence that you failed to communicate well enough.
