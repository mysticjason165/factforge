Stop Switching Tasks Long Enough to Finish One Piece

FactForge · 1 min read

You open a document, remember an email, check a message, and return to the document without knowing where you stopped. The focus manuscript describes this kind of scattered work as a reason to protect a defined task from unnecessary interruption.

Choose a small unit of progress rather than a vague instruction to be productive. Keep a place to capture unrelated reminders so you do not have to act on every thought. Close material that is not needed for the current step, while keeping essential communication available for your situation.

At the end of the session, record what is complete and where to resume. Do not measure success only by how long you sat there. A finished paragraph, checked calculation, or resolved question gives the session a visible result. If the method does not fit, adjust the task size before assuming you need more willpower.
