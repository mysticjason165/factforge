Give Yourself Permission to Leave an Online Argument

FactForge · 1 min read

An online reply can feel like a demand for an immediate defense. Before responding, ask what a useful outcome would look like. Clarifying a misunderstanding is different from trying to persuade someone who keeps changing the subject.

You can correct a factual point once, decline further conversation, or use the platform's blocking and reporting tools. Save relevant evidence if the behavior includes threats or targeted harassment. Avoid posting private information or escalating the exchange in an attempt to win it.

After leaving, choose an activity that does not keep pulling you back to the thread. You do not owe every stranger access to your time. Serious threats may require help beyond platform moderation. For ordinary hostile exchanges, the useful boundary can be simple: you are allowed to stop participating even when the other person insists that leaving proves something about you.
