Check an Unexpected Message Before Following Its Link

FactForge · 1 min read

A message can look familiar and still lead somewhere unsafe. Logos and urgent language are not enough to verify the sender. The FTC recommends contacting an organization through a channel you already know is genuine when a message seems questionable.

If a notice says your account needs attention, open the known app or type the official address yourself rather than using the unexpected link. Check whether the issue appears there. Do not enter sensitive information merely because the message creates a short deadline.

Keep the message available if you need to report it, but avoid experimenting with its attachments. A brief pause can give you room to verify the request independently. The important habit is not memorizing every possible scam design. It is refusing to let an unfamiliar message provide both the alarming claim and the only route you use to check that claim.
