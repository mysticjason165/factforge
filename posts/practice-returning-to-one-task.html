Practice Returning to One Task

FactForge · 1 min read

Concentration does not have to mean never noticing anything else. A more workable practice is recognizing that your attention moved and choosing where to place it next. That gives you something to do after an interruption.

Choose a small task and remove the most obvious competing activity. If another idea appears, write a short reminder rather than following it immediately. Then return to the exact sentence, calculation, or action you left. Keep the session short enough that beginning does not feel like a major commitment.

At the end, ask what made returning easier. Was the task too vague? Did you need a quieter place? Were messages genuinely urgent? Your answers can shape the next session. This is a working habit inspired by the focus manuscript, not a treatment for an attention disorder or evidence that every distraction is under voluntary control.
