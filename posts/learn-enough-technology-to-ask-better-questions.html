Learn Enough Technology to Ask Better Questions

FactForge · 1 min read

You do not need to become an engineer to ask useful questions about a digital tool. Understanding basic ideas such as files, permissions, storage, and inputs can make a confusing system easier to discuss.

Start with a task you already perform. Trace where the information comes from, what the program changes, and where the result goes. If a tool asks for access, ask what that permission enables and whether the task needs it. Use official documentation when checking how a specific product works.

Keep a small glossary in your own words and revise it as you learn. Avoid pretending a quick lesson makes you an expert. The aim is enough understanding to notice missing explanations and seek the right help. Technical literacy becomes useful when it improves a real decision, not only when you can repeat more specialized terms.
