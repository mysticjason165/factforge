Give a Short Explanation with a Clear Point

FactForge · 1 min read

Speaking clearly begins before you choose the exact words. Decide what the listener needs to understand or do after the conversation. That gives the explanation a center instead of a sequence of loosely related thoughts.

State the point, give the most relevant reason, and use one example if it helps. Leave room for a question rather than filling every pause. In a work conversation, explain what decision or response you need and when it matters.

Practice aloud and notice where you lose the thread. Remove background that the listener does not need yet. Clear speaking does not require sounding formal or never hesitating. It requires making the relationship between the pieces understandable. A short explanation that supports a useful next step often does more than a long speech that leaves the listener unsure what you wanted from them.
