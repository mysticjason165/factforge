Read the Axes Before Reacting to a Chart

FactForge · 1 min read

A chart can make a small difference look dramatic or a large one look ordinary. Before interpreting the shape, read the labels, units, and scale. Find out what each axis represents and which period is included.

Imagine two bars that begin near the top of a shortened scale. Their visual difference may look larger than it would on a scale beginning at zero. That does not automatically make the chart dishonest, but it changes how you should describe the comparison.

Look for missing context such as sample size, source, or excluded dates. If a headline claims a trend, check whether the chart contains enough observations to show it. A useful graph should make information easier to understand. Slow down when the emotional impression arrives before you can explain what the plotted values actually mean.
