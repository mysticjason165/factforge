Plan for the Habits You Want to Keep

FactForge · 1 min read

A short-term effort and a long-term routine can look very different. Before making a major change, ask which parts you would still want to do during a busy month. That question can reveal a plan that depends on temporary enthusiasm.

NIDDK treats maintenance as part of behavior change rather than an afterthought. Identify the practical supports behind the habit: available food, an accessible place to move, or a schedule that leaves time. Consider what happens during travel, illness, or an unusually demanding week.

Create a smaller fallback version rather than an all-or-nothing rule. Returning after a disruption matters more than pretending disruptions can be eliminated. If weight management is part of the goal, get individual guidance rather than assuming one routine will produce the same outcome for everyone. A useful plan should be livable after the initial deadline has passed.
