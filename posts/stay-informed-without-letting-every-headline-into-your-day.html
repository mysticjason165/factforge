Stay Informed Without Letting Every Headline Into Your Day

FactForge · 1 min read

Being informed does not require checking every update as soon as it appears. Start by deciding what information you need for your responsibilities and interests. That creates a reason to open the news—and a reason to stop.

Choose a small set of sources, check the original reporting when a claim matters, and give reading a defined place in the day. The CDC suggests breaks from news and social media as one way to manage stress. A break can coexist with serious attention to important events.

When a story leaves you unsettled, separate what you learned from what you can do. Sometimes there is a practical action; sometimes the next useful step is to return later for verified information. Repeatedly refreshing the same uncertainty does not create an answer. Build an information routine that leaves room for the rest of your life.
