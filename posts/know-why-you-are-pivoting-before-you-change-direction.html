Know Why You Are Pivoting Before You Change Direction

FactForge · 1 min read

Changing direction can be sensible when new information challenges the original plan. It can also become a way to avoid the uncomfortable middle of learning. Before pivoting, describe what is no longer working and how you know.

Separate evidence from boredom. Repeated user confusion may suggest the product needs a change; one frustrating afternoon may suggest you need a break or help. Identify what the new direction is expected to solve and which parts of the existing work remain useful.

Try a limited change when possible before abandoning everything. Record what you learned so the next attempt does not repeat the same uncertainty. Adaptability is not constant motion for its own sake. A deliberate pivot has a reason you can explain and a manageable next step, rather than only the temporary excitement of beginning something new.
