Ask What a Statistic Is Actually Measuring

FactForge · 1 min read

A statistic can look precise while answering a different question from the one you care about. Before reacting to a percentage, find out what was counted and who was included. The definition matters as much as the number.

Suppose a course reports that most surveyed graduates found work. Ask how many people replied, what counted as work, and when the survey happened. Those questions do not prove the claim is false. They clarify what you can reasonably conclude from it.

Look for the original report rather than relying only on a graphic or headline. Keep uncertainty visible when details are missing. A useful number should help you understand a situation more clearly. If the measurement remains unclear, you can postpone the conclusion instead of choosing between believing the claim completely and dismissing all statistics as meaningless.
