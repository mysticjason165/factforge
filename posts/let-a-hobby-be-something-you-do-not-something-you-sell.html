Let a Hobby Be Something You Do, Not Something You Sell

FactForge · 1 min read

A hobby can lose its appeal when every session becomes content, a product, or evidence of progress. Making something without an audience can leave room to experiment in ways that a public project does not.

Choose an activity you want to try and keep the first commitment small. Draw a page, learn a song fragment, or work on a simple craft. Use what you already have when practical. You do not need to build a brand around the interest before discovering whether you enjoy it.

Notice the experience itself rather than how well it compares with someone else's result. You may eventually share or sell the work, but that choice can wait. Not every useful part of life needs to generate income. A private, unfinished experiment is still a legitimate way to spend your time.
