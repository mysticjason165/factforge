Ask Who Is Missing from a Success Story

FactForge · 1 min read

A collection of success stories shows what happened to the people selected for the collection. It does not necessarily show what happened to everyone who tried. That missing group matters when you are deciding what an example can prove.

Suppose a program highlights a few graduates with impressive results. Ask how many people participated and how outcomes were gathered. Were unsuccessful participants included? Were results verified? Those questions do not deny anyone's achievement; they clarify whether the examples describe a wider pattern.

Use stories for ideas and context, then seek broader information before making a major commitment. A vivid example can be memorable without being representative. Careful reading leaves room to admire the person while withholding a general conclusion that the available evidence cannot support. Inspiration and prediction are different uses of the same story.
