Recognize the Pressure in a Suspicious Request

FactForge · 1 min read

A scam attempt often asks you to act before you have time to verify the story. The request may sound helpful, frightening, or unusually rewarding. Look at what you are being asked to do, not only the emotion the message creates.

Separate the claimed identity from the requested action. If someone says they represent a company, verify through a known official channel. Do not let the caller choose the only person you can consult or the only website you can use. The FTC's phishing guidance supports independent verification of unexpected requests.

Discuss the situation with someone you trust when practical. Being targeted is not proof of poor judgment, and embarrassment should not prevent asking for help. A useful defense is a repeatable checking process that still works when the story changes, rather than confidence that you would never believe a convincing message.
