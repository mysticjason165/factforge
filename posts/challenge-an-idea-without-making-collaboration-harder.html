Challenge an Idea Without Making Collaboration Harder

FactForge · 1 min read

Independent thinking is useful when it improves the decision, not simply when it makes a meeting more difficult. You can question a plan while treating the people involved with respect.

Name the specific concern and explain the evidence or experience behind it. Offer an alternative when you can, and identify what would change your mind. Ask whether the disagreement concerns the goal, the method, or a missing piece of information. Those are different problems and may need different conversations.

Listen for constraints you did not know about. Being willing to revise a position is compatible with having a strong point of view. Leadership should not confuse hostility with originality or compliance with cooperation. A useful challenge leaves the group better able to understand the choice, even when the final decision is not the one you initially preferred.
