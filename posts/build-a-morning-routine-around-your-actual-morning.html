Build a Morning Routine Around Your Actual Morning

FactForge · 1 min read

A morning routine should fit the time between waking up and your first real obligation. Copying someone else's long routine can create a new source of pressure before the day has properly started.

List what must happen: getting dressed, eating if that suits you, taking prescribed medicine, traveling, or preparing a child for school. Then choose one small optional action that would make the morning less rushed. It might be reviewing the day's first task or leaving your phone aside while you get ready.

Prepare whatever you can the night before and keep a shorter version for difficult mornings. Missing an optional step does not mean the routine failed. Its purpose is to support your responsibilities, not to test your discipline. After a week, keep the parts that helped and remove the parts that mostly looked impressive on paper.
