Judge an Exercise Game by the Movement, Not the Score

FactForge · 1 min read

An exercise game can be engaging, but a high score does not automatically describe the amount or intensity of movement. Different games ask for different actions, and players may use different levels of effort.

Look at what you actually do during a session. The CDC describes intensity in terms of effort, including the talk test, rather than entertainment value. Make sure the area is clear and follow the device's safety instructions. Stop if the experience causes dizziness, discomfort, or another concerning reaction.

Consider whether the game helps you maintain an activity you enjoy. It may be one part of a routine without replacing every other kind of movement. You do not need to prove that it is better than all traditional exercise. The useful question is whether this particular activity is suitable, practical, and worth returning to in your circumstances.
