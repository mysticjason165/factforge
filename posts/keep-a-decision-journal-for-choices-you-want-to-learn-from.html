Keep a Decision Journal for Choices You Want to Learn From

FactForge · 1 min read

A decision journal records what you knew before the outcome arrived. Without that record, it is easy to remember a prediction as more certain or obvious than it really was.

Write the choice, the available options, your main reason, and the uncertainty you cannot resolve. Add what would make you reconsider. Keep the entry short enough to use before ordinary decisions, such as choosing a project format or committing to a weekly schedule.

Review it after enough time has passed to learn something. Separate the quality of the process from luck in the outcome. A reasonable decision can turn out badly, and a careless one can happen to work. The journal is useful when it improves your questions next time. It should not become a system for repeatedly blaming yourself for information you did not have.
