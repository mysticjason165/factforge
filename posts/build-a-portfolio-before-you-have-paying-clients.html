Build a Portfolio Before You Have Paying Clients

FactForge · 1 min read

The awkward part of finding your first client is being asked to show work you have not been paid to do yet. A small collection of clearly labeled sample projects can answer that question without inventing a client history.

Pick a service and create three examples that show different challenges. For website design, you could build a fictional café page, a student club page, and a writer's portfolio. Explain the purpose, the choices you made, and what works. Label each as a personal project rather than implying an organization hired you.

Keep the presentation short enough to review quickly. One strong screenshot and a useful explanation beat a folder of unfinished experiments. When approaching a potential client, share the example closest to their needs. The portfolio's job is to make your ability visible, not to make your experience look bigger than it is.
