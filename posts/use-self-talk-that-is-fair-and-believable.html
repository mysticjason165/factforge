Use Self-Talk That Is Fair and Believable

FactForge · 1 min read

When something goes wrong, a harsh inner comment can turn one mistake into a sweeping story. Saying you always fail goes beyond what a single event can show. Replacing it with an equally sweeping compliment may not feel believable either.

Describe the situation more precisely. You missed a deadline, misunderstood a question, or avoided a difficult task. Then identify what is still possible: explain the delay, ask for clarification, or complete one part. Specific language leaves room for action without denying the problem.

Try writing the response you would offer someone you respect in the same situation. Keep it honest and practical. You do not have to feel confident before taking the next useful step. Fair self-talk is not pretending every outcome is good; it is refusing to make the explanation harsher and broader than the evidence supports.
