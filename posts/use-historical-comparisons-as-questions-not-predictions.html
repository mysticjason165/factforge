Use Historical Comparisons as Questions, Not Predictions

FactForge · 1 min read

A present-day event may resemble something from history without following the same path. A comparison can reveal a useful question, but it can also hide differences if the resemblance becomes the whole argument.

Identify the specific feature being compared. Is it a policy, a technology, an economic pressure, or a pattern of public response? Then ask what differs in the institutions, available information, and circumstances. Avoid assuming a shared label means the situations are identical.

Read historical sources with attention to their context and distinguish a historian's interpretation from a settled fact. When using the comparison in writing, explain both its value and its limits. History can widen the range of possibilities you consider. It cannot turn a complicated present into a script whose ending is already guaranteed.
