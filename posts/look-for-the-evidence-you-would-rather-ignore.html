Look for the Evidence You Would Rather Ignore

FactForge · 1 min read

When you like an idea, supporting examples can become easier to notice than conflicting ones. A deliberate search for disagreement can help you examine the decision without pretending you are free from preferences.

Write down what would count against your current view. If you think an audience needs a new product, what response would suggest otherwise? Ask a question that allows an honest answer instead of inviting people to praise the plan.

Evaluate the conflicting evidence by the same standard you apply to support. One negative comment is not automatically decisive, just as one compliment is not proof. The exercise is useful when it changes what you investigate next. Good judgment does not require having no favorite answer; it requires giving inconvenient information a fair chance to affect the answer you eventually choose.
