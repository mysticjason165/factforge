Turn a Vague Problem into Conditions You Can Check

FactForge · 1 min read

A problem such as choosing the best project tool can feel impossible because best has no defined meaning. List the conditions the tool needs to meet before comparing attractive features.

For a shared assignment, the conditions might include opening on everyone's device, allowing the required file type, and fitting the budget. Separate requirements from preferences. A tool that fails a requirement should not win merely because its interface looks nicer.

Test the remaining choices with a small example that resembles the real task. Record where a tradeoff remains rather than pretending one option is perfect. This kind of everyday logic keeps the decision connected to its purpose. It also makes the choice easier to explain to another person, because the reasons are visible instead of being hidden inside a general feeling that something seems better.
