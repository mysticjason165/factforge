Separate What You Know from What You Assume

FactForge · 1 min read

A disagreement can become easier to examine when you separate an observation from the explanation attached to it. Someone arrived late is an observation. They do not respect anyone's time is an interpretation that requires more information.

Write the claim in plain language and ask what evidence would support it. Consider at least one other explanation. This does not require treating every possibility as equally likely; it simply prevents the first story from becoming a fact without examination.

When the decision matters, identify what additional information you could reasonably obtain. You may still need to act before everything is known, but you can state the uncertainty honestly. Careful thinking is not endless hesitation. It is understanding which parts of the conclusion rest on evidence and which parts depend on assumptions you may need to revise.
