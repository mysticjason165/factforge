Write an Outreach Message Someone Can Understand Quickly

FactForge · 1 min read

A useful outreach message tells someone why you contacted them, what you can help with, and what the next step would be. Long introductions about your ambition can hide those three things.

Research enough to identify a relevant need without pretending you know the person's entire business. You might notice that a public event flyer is hard to read on a phone. Explain the observation politely, show one relevant example of your work, and offer a short conversation about a defined service.

Keep the message personal and easy to decline. Do not scrape private contact information or repeatedly pursue someone who has said no. Record which kinds of requests lead to useful conversations and revise your offer accordingly. Good outreach starts with relevance and respect; sending more copies of a vague pitch does not fix the pitch itself.
