Verify an Image by Its Context, Not Just Its Appearance

FactForge · 1 min read

An unusual image may be authentic, edited, generated, or simply attached to the wrong story. Looking for visual oddities can raise questions, but it is not enough to settle where an image came from or what it shows.

Find the earliest reliable source you can locate and compare the caption, date, and setting. Look for independent reporting when the claim matters. A screenshot of a screenshot offers less context than the original publication, and a confident caption is still a claim that needs checking.

If you cannot verify the image, say so rather than assigning a certainty you do not have. Avoid sharing it as evidence while the basic context remains unresolved. The useful skill is tracing the claim around the picture. Even a real photograph can mislead when the date, location, or explanation has been changed.
